## Headline

**No linguistically-grounded variant reverses the official ranking.** BPE remains
ahead of MoVoC-Tok on every motivated variant in both languages. The single
reversal observed anywhere in this analysis (Ge'ez, +1.40) comes from blanket
±1 tolerance — the *least* linguistically constrained criterion tested, and one
the reporting policy already excludes as unofficial.

This analysis therefore yields **no additional evidence supporting
morphology-aware segmentation** on precision for these two languages. That is
the finding, reported as found.

## Variant A — abugida-aware matching

Ethiopic is an abugida: one character encodes a consonant *and* a vowel. At a
morpheme join the final consonant of one morpheme and the initial vowel of the
next can land in a **single character**, so the true boundary falls *inside* a
character and no character-offset criterion can place it exactly. This is the
standard motivation for relaxing exact matching here.

**A3 fusion-aware** encodes exactly that and nothing more. Ethiopic syllables
occupy U+1200–U+137F in families of 8: `(cp − 0x1200) // 8` is the consonant
family, `(cp − 0x1200) % 8` the vowel order. A predicted boundary `p` is credited
against gold `g` when

- `p == g`, or
- `|p − g| == 1` **and** the character straddled by the pair is Ethiopic **and**
  carries a non-zero vowel order (a fused, non-base form).

The vowel-order condition is what makes A3 narrower than A2: credit is given
only where the orthography actually fuses. Base forms (vowel order 0) and
non-Ethiopic characters get none. Matching is greedy nearest-first and 1-to-1, so
one gold boundary cannot absorb two predictions.

| Variant | Amh MoVoC-Tok | Amh BPE | Δ | Ge'ez MoVoC-Tok | Ge'ez BPE | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A1 exact (official) | 24.01 | 24.29 | −0.28 | 35.40 | 36.80 | −1.40 |
| A2 ±1 tolerance | 59.75 | 60.98 | −1.23 | **64.34** | 62.94 | **+1.40** |
| A3 fusion-aware | 48.52 | 49.17 | −0.65 | 49.10 | 51.02 | −1.92 |

A1 reproduces the official values exactly (24.0/24.3, 35.4/36.8), confirming the
harness is anchored to the official computation.

**The decisive observation is the A2/A3 contrast.** Restricting the ±1 credit to
genuine fusion points removes roughly 11–15 points of the tolerance gain, which
confirms that most off-by-one agreement is *not* fusion — it is generic
near-miss. And under the linguistically constrained criterion Ge'ez does not
flip: MoVoC-Tok goes from −1.40 (exact) to **−1.92** (fusion-aware), a *wider*
deficit. The Ge'ez reversal at A2 is therefore an artefact of unconstrained
tolerance, not evidence of morphological alignment.

### Supplementary: Ge'ez under ±1 boundary tolerance

**Linguistically Grounded Sensitivity Analysis — not an Official Table 4
Reproduction.** The official Table 4 reproduction remains based on the
exact-match precision metric implemented in the released repository, where Ge'ez
is MoVoC-Tok 35.4 / BPE 36.8.

Under ±1 boundary tolerance, the Ge'ez ranking reverses:

| Ge'ez (±1 tolerance) | Precision |
|---|---:|
| MoVoC-Tok | **64.34** |
| BPE | 62.94 |
| **Difference** | **+1.40** |

A substantial proportion of segmentation disagreements occur exactly one
character from a gold morpheme boundary: 54.52% of MoVoC-Tok's predicted Ge'ez
boundaries are off-by-one, and exact + off-by-1 + off-by-2 covers ~99.7% of all
predictions (Variant E). Relaxing the offset by one character is therefore the
single largest lever on Ge'ez precision, and under it MoVoC-Tok leads.

**Interpretive limit — the fusion hypothesis was tested and does not explain this
reversal.** The natural reading of a ±1 effect in Ge'ez script is abugida fusion:
one orthographic character encodes a consonant–vowel combination, so a morpheme
boundary can fall *inside* a fused character and no character-offset criterion
can place it exactly. Variant A3 was built to test exactly that, crediting an
off-by-one **only** where the straddled character is Ethiopic and carries a
non-zero vowel order — that is, only at genuine fusion points.

Ge'ez does not flip under that test. MoVoC-Tok moves from −1.40 (exact) to
**−1.92** (fusion-aware): the deficit *widens*. Restricting the credit to real
fusion points also removes ~15 points of the tolerance gain, showing that most
off-by-one agreement in Ge'ez is generic near-miss rather than fusion.

The +1.40 is therefore a real property of the ±1 metric, but it cannot be
attributed to abugida fusion — the fusion-constrained measurement rules that
mechanism out as its cause. It should be read as **weak, unexplained supporting
evidence** that MoVoC-Tok may better capture Ge'ez morphology once boundary
offsets are tolerated, with the caveat that the mechanism is not the one the
orthography would predict, and that n = 173 words is small. It is not evidence
that MoVoC-Tok outperforms BPE on Ge'ez precision, and it does not qualify the
official result.

## Variant B — root preservation

Gold boundaries were split into those delimiting the **root span** and those
delimiting affixes, scoring each by recall (how many such gold boundaries the
tokenizer actually produced).

| Language | Tokenizer | Root boundaries | Root recall | Affix boundaries | Affix recall |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Amharic | MoVoC-Tok | 208,864 | 24.29% | 65,207 | 12.85% |
| Amharic | BPE | 208,864 | 25.73% | 65,207 | 12.96% |
| Ge'ez | MoVoC-Tok | 277 | 53.10% | 0 | n/a |
| Ge'ez | BPE | 277 | 56.20% | 0 | n/a |

**The Ge'ez affix cells are structurally empty, not zero-scoring.** Every Ge'ez
word in the annotation is `prefix-root-suffix` (85), `root-suffix` (69) or
`prefix-root` (19), so *every* gold boundary is a root-span edge and the affix
partition is empty by construction. The `0.0` in `root_boundary_scores.csv` is a
division-guard, not a measurement; it must not be read as an affix score. The
root/affix split is informative for Amharic only, where clitics create
boundaries not adjacent to the root.

For Amharic, both tokenizers recover root boundaries about twice as often as
affix boundaries — root edges are the easier target for both — and BPE leads on
both partitions. No root-preservation advantage for MoVoC-Tok is visible.

## Variant C — morphologically complex words

If MoVoC-Tok's constrained merges help, the effect should grow with morphological
complexity.

| Subset | Amh MoVoC-Tok | Amh BPE | Δ | Ge'ez MoVoC-Tok | Ge'ez BPE | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| all multi-morphemic | 24.01 | 24.29 | −0.28 | 35.40 | 36.80 | −1.40 |
| ≥3 morphemes | 28.50 | 28.49 | **+0.01** | 38.43 | 40.77 | −2.34 |
| ≥4 morphemes | 33.99 | 33.79 | **+0.20** | — | — | — |

Amharic shows the predicted *direction*: MoVoC-Tok's deficit closes at ≥3 and
turns marginally positive at ≥4 (n = 21,405). The magnitude (+0.20) is far too
small to claim as a result, but the monotone trend across three nested subsets is
the one signal in this analysis pointing the way the paper's hypothesis predicts.
Ge'ez moves the other way, and has no ≥4 subset.

Both tokenizers score substantially higher on complex words, so the trend is
partly the subsets being easier for everyone.

## Variant E — error typology

Percentages are of each tokenizer's own predicted boundaries. The first four
categories partition all predictions; `root_split` and `affix_merge` are
overlapping diagnostics.

| Language | Tokenizer | Exact hit | Off-by-1 | Off-by-2 | Other miss | Root split | Affix merge |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Amharic | MoVoC-Tok | 24.01 | 41.89 | 16.91 | 17.20 | 19.82 | 11.58 |
| Amharic | BPE | 24.29 | 42.62 | 16.37 | 16.72 | 19.47 | 11.08 |
| Ge'ez | MoVoC-Tok | 35.40 | 54.52 | 9.82 | 0.26 | 10.59 | 0.00 |
| Ge'ez | BPE | 36.80 | 53.81 | 9.14 | 0.25 | 9.64 | 0.00* |

\* Ge'ez `affix_merge` is 0.00 for both arms by the same structural emptiness
described under Variant B.

The dominant error is **off-by-one**, not gross mis-segmentation: 42% of Amharic
and 54% of Ge'ez predicted boundaries land one character from a gold boundary.
In Ge'ez, exact + off-by-1 + off-by-2 accounts for ~99.7% of predictions, so
essentially every boundary is within two characters of a true one. This is the
character-offset severity effect documented in `REPRODUCTION_STATUS.md` §4, and
Variant A quantifies how much of it is genuine abugida fusion: **less than half**.

Critically, the error profiles of the two tokenizers are near-identical
throughout — differences are ≤0.8pp on every category. Whatever is suppressing
precision affects MoVoC-Tok and BPE **equally**, which is consistent with an
annotation-to-surface projection issue rather than a tokenizer property.

## What this does and does not support

**Does not support:**

- Any claim that MoVoC-Tok beats BPE on precision in Amharic or Ge'ez. It does
  not, under any linguistically motivated variant tested.
- Reading the Ge'ez A2 (±1) reversal as evidence for MoVoC-Tok. The constrained
  fusion-aware variant reverses that reversal.
- Any change to the official Table 4 values or ranking.

**Does support:**

- The precision gap versus published values (§4 of `REPRODUCTION_STATUS.md`)
  being an artefact of exact character-offset matching against citation-form
  annotations, affecting both tokenizers equally.
- Fusion accounting for a real but minority share of that artefact — Variant A
  isolates it and finds generic near-miss dominates.
- A weak, complexity-dependent trend for Amharic (Variant C) that is directionally
  consistent with the paper's hypothesis but too small to claim.

## Reproducibility

All variants are deterministic and derive from the audited inputs. Fusion-aware
matching depends only on Unicode codepoint arithmetic, with no language-specific
lists, tuned thresholds or hand-curated exceptions. No new metric was invented:
A1 is the official metric, A2 was already reported in the re-audit, B/C are
subset restrictions of the official metric, D is standard span F1/Jaccard, and E
is a descriptive error partition carrying no score.
